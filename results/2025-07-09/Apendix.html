Relatedness or coancestry

Relatedness between individuals consists on the sharing of alleles identical by descent (IBD). It is not observable directly, but it can be estimated from genotypes. Between two diploid individuals there are different ways of sharing any pair of alleles IBD. Cockerham (1971) defined 15 probabilities, for the 15 different patterns of IBD in one locus between two diploid individuals. Jacquard (1970) used only 9 such parameters, because he did not distinguish paternal from maternal alleles. In the absence of inbreeding, the two alleles in one individual cannot be IBD, an the number of parameters reduces to three, commonly refered to as \(K_2\), \(K_1\) and \(K_0\), which are the probabilities of the two individuals sharing 0, 1 or the two alleles IBD. They correspond to the last three of Jacquard’s probabilities, \(\Delta_7\), \(\Delta_8\) and \(\Delta_9\), respectively.

The coefficient of coancestry, \(\Theta_{xy}\) is defined as the probability of a random allele of one individual being IBD to a random allele of the same locus in the other individual (Lynch and Walsh 1998), and it is a function of Jacquard’s condensed coefficients of identity:

\[ \Theta_{xy} = \Delta_1 + \frac{1}{2}(\Delta_3 + \Delta_5 + \Delta_7) + \frac{1}{4}\Delta_8 \]

It is also called coefficient of kinship, and it is equal to the inbreeding coefficient of an offspring between the two individuals. Another measure of relatedness is the proportion of homologous alleles shared between two individuals due to IBD from a common ancestor (Hedrick and Lacy 2014):

\[ r_{yx} = \Delta_1 + \Delta_7 + \frac{3}{4}(\Delta_3 + \Delta_5) + \frac{1}{2}\Delta_8 \]

The output of ngsRelate (Hanghøj, K. and Moltke, I. and Andersen, P.A, and Manica, A. and Korneliussen, T.S. 2019) includes the ML estimates of all 9 Jacquard’s condensed coefficients of identity, and the relatedness sensu Hedrick and Lacy (2014), among other things.

Sequence differences

The ngsrelate output includes the two-individuals 2D SFS of every comparison. They are 9 numbers reporting the fraction of all sites used in that comparison where each individual had each of the 3 possible genotypes in a biallelic site. From those numbers I can derive the fraction of sites where genotypes are different between the two individuals. Another measure of genetic distance is the expected difference upon randomly drawing one allele from each individual. So, a site where one is heterozygous and the other is homozygous would contribute 0.5 to the difference, which is the chance of picking the different alleles. The same is true if both are heterozygous. This is computed in the Dist column of both data frames.
