\(F_{ST}\) from estimated ancestral allele frequencies

On 2024-03-11 we run NGSadmix on variable sites identified from mappings to the RAD loci. K = 2 was determined as a good fit. The program not only yields the ancestry proportions of every individual (the \(N \times K\) \(Q\) matrix) but also the estimated allele frequencies in the ancestral populations. Thus, we can estimate the \(F_{ST}\) among those presumed ancestral populations. I would do this just to check that the \(F_{ST}\) estimated on 2024-10-10 between what we call C. farionis (most abundant in Langfjordvatn) and C. pseudofarionis (most abundant in Suohpatjavri), namely 0.12, is consistent with the NGSadmix analysis.

To estimate \(F_{ST}\) from estimated allele frequencies, I assume Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium in all 315642 loci included in the NGSadmix analysis. Unfortunately, the number of chromosomes used in each locus to estimate allele frequencies is unknown. At most, all 90 individuals had good data, but most probably, the amount of data is variable across sites. I believe smaller sample sizes produced downwardly biased estimates. Thus, if I specify the largest possible sample sizes, I will get the largest estimates.

In any case, the (at most) 90 current individuals represent different sample sizes of the two ancestral populations assumed when \(K=2\). I assume that the two ancestries are represented by population sizes proportional to the overall current fraction of the genomes inherited from them.

source('formulas.R')
K2 <- read.table('../2024-03-11/NGSadmix/ARCTIC.K2.fopt.gz',

NumRep <- 250
if (file.exists('FST.Rds')) {

   FST <- list(
      estimates = sapply(1:5, function(x) WC84w(K2, n.max / x)),
      bootstrap = sapply(1:5, function(x) {
         replicate(NumRep, WC84w(K2[sample(1:dim(K2)[1], 5000, replace = TRUE),], n.max / x))
      })

}

Grey dots are bootstrap pseudo-replicates, obtained with only 5000 sites.

The two ancestral populations estimated with NGSadmix on 2024-03-11 seem to have diverged to an \(F_{ST} \leq 0.176\), which is higher than the 0.12 value obtained in 2024-10-10 between current populations. The difference is unlikely to be due to recent admixture, because on 2024-10-10 only non-admixed individuals were used. I assume the estimate here is higher for two reasons: first, because the algorithm in NGSadmix must maximize the differences between hypotheized ancestral populations. And second, because estimates with realSFS (in 2024-10-10) may take into account the variable and limited sample size across sites, while I naively assume all loci have data in either all or a constant fraction of individuals. The smaller the fraction, the lower the estimate.

Nucleotide diversity from the SFS

PiTajima <- function(x, folded = TRUE){
   n <- length(x) - 1  # the SFS includes invariable sites and 0 values for i > n/2
   a <- 1:(n/2)
   Theta <- (2/(n * (n-1))) * sum(x[2:(n/2 + 1)] * a * (n - a)) / sum(x)
   return(Theta)
}

Both SFSs show a deficit of singletons, which must be due to missed heterozygous sites or confusion of singletons with sequencing errors. And they show an excess of high frequency SNPs, which must be the cause of such high \(\pi\) values. Note that I originally included very low-coverage sites in the estimates. Because I suspected the lack of singletons could be due to such low-coverage sites hiding heterozygotes, I re-estimated the SFSs in the 2024-10-10 folder, using a minimum coverage of 4 per individual, and a minimum number of individuals equal to 4/5 the total number, in each population. The results are essentially the same, with a small increase of heterozygosity.

The SFSs suggest that invariable sites (first category of the SFS) are about 4.19 or 4.93 times more abundant than variable sites, in C. pseudofarionis (Suopatjavri) and C. farionis (Langfjordvatn), respectively.

Assuming the lack of singletons is an artifact, the remaining question is why there is an excess of high frequency SNPs. Note that part of that excess is shared or correlated between the two populations (see 2024-10-10). One possibility is balancing selection.
